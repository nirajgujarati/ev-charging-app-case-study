# 09. Portfolio Narrative and Interview Talking Points

## Positioning
Present this as **customer-problem research and solution design**, which is the core of a Forward Deployed Engineer role: understand a user's pain, research the market, design a solution within technical limits. State clearly that it is a design, not a shipped product.

## Suggested page structure
1. Problem (01)
2. Research: market and competitors (02, 03)
3. User research: 14 pain points (04). This is the strongest section.
4. Solution design (05)
5. Technical feasibility (06)
6. Gap analysis and risks (08)
7. Next steps

## 60-second pitch
"Indian EV owners keep three or four charging apps because none shows a trustworthy charger status. I mapped the market (1 crore+ EVs), the competitors, and 14 real complaints from Reddit, Team-BHP and app reviews. My concept uses payment as check-in, so status stays accurate without manual entry, plus car-based time estimates and slot booking. I also identified the weak points: it needs user density and self-listed data is not machine-verified."

## Likely questions and answers
- **Why not just use Google Maps?** It commoditizes find-and-status, so the app must compete on trust, booking and payment, not listings.
- **How do you get data without CPO deals?** Self-listing, payment events, and user check-ins, labelled "user reported". Long term: OCPP hardware, OCPI, and UEI.
- **What is the riskiest assumption?** That enough users and owners adopt it for crowdsourced status to be reliable.
- **How did you validate?** Public user reports only. No interviews or prototype yet. Next step is a small prototype and 5-10 owner and driver interviews.
- **What would you build first?** Map, listing with GPS and photo, vehicle select, check-in flow.

## Do and don't
- Do show sources and label estimates.
- Do state what is not validated.
- Don't claim market share, revenue or user numbers for the concept.
