# 01. The Problem

## Context
India has crossed 1 crore (10 million) registered EVs (VAHAN, July 2026). Public charging has grown fast, but the experience of using it has not kept pace.

## The core problem
Charging information is fragmented and not trustworthy.
- Each charge point operator (CPO) has its own app and its own wallet.
- An app can show a charger as "available" when it is broken or offline.
- Users cannot tell how long they will wait or how long a charge will take.
- Tier-2 and tier-3 cities have thin coverage and little data.

One user on r/CarsIndia described the result: "I now have 3-4 EV charging apps on my phone because I don't fully trust any one of them." (https://www.reddit.com/r/CarsIndia/comments/1r2r276/)

## Who has the problem
- **Car owners** on highway trips and in cities, who need to know a charger works before driving to it.
- **Two-wheeler owners**, who face connector and app compatibility issues (see pain point 12).
- **Station owners** in smaller cities, who have chargers but no visibility or booking tools (concept hypothesis, not yet interviewed).

## Questions this study asks
1. Does a market for a better charging app exist, and how big is it?
2. Who already competes, and at what scale?
3. What do real users complain about?
4. Can a new app solve those complaints with data it can actually get?
5. What is the honest weak point of the idea?
