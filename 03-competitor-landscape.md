# 03. Competitor Landscape

Two kinds of players exist: **aggregators** (show many networks) and **CPOs** (own and run chargers, with live data on their own network).

All figures are claims reported at research time (25 Oct 2026). "Not public" means the research did not find a figure.

## Aggregators
| Player | Scale | Funding | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| ElectricPe (Bengaluru) | 25,000+ chargers listed; ~1L downloads; also claims 10,000 live points | ~USD 11.3M | Supplies real-time data to Google Maps |
| Statiq | 10,000+ chargers, 1,014 cities | USD 43M+ (Shell Ventures, Tenacity) | Source: https://entrackr.com/news/ev-charging-startup-statiq-raises-18-mn-led-by-tenacity-ventures-11127182 |
| Kazam | Not public in this research | Not public | Aggregator plus "host your own charger" model; Google Maps partner |
| Bolt.Earth | Claims 100K+ chargers | Not public | Majority are private or peer-to-peer, not public fast chargers |
| PlugShare (global benchmark) | 587K+ stations, 10L+ monthly users | Not public | Weak in India |

## Google Maps
Google Maps shows basic find-and-status for 8,000+ stations through partnerships with ElectricPe, Statiq, Kazam and Ather. Source: https://blog.google/intl/en-in/products/explore-communicate/six-ways-were-enabling-more-efficient-and-sustainable-journeys-on-google-maps-in-india-powered-by-ai-and-local-partners/

## Observations
1. The scale gap is large. A new entrant cannot win on number of listings.
2. Live occupancy is strong only **inside one CPO's own network**. No one gives reliable live status across all networks.
3. Wait-time prediction exists at Tata (wait timer) and is rare elsewhere.
4. Hosting models already exist (Kazam, Bolt.Earth), so self-listing is proven, not novel.
